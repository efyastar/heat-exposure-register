What it costs

Cost per case comes from published data. How often heat illness happens is your estimate — change it and watch the totals move while the order of your sites stays put.

Each site is scaled by how much hotter it runs than average, then costed at a blend of $757 per emergency-room visit and $14,900 per hospital admission (HCUP 2020, via the Center for American Progress).

Where these numbers come from

Heat readings are 2 m ambient air temperature at street resolution from FortyGuard's Temperature API — the air a person actually stands in, not ground-surface temperature and not an airport weather station miles away. Each site's figure counts the hours it spends above the danger threshold across the study window.

Why this tool measures exposure instead of simulating tree planting

The original plan was a cooling simulator — move a canopy slider, predict the temperature drop. We tested whether that was defensible before building it. It was not.

We joined 7,347 tiles of measured heat to land cover from OpenStreetMap — 38,649 building footprints, 41,464 road segments, 810 green spaces — and trained gradient-boosted models under spatial block cross-validation, holding out whole districts so no model could memorise a neighbourhood and then be graded on its next-door tile.

Coordinates alone reached R² 0.85. Adding every land-cover feature changed it by −0.009. Building fraction, green fraction and tree count all scored zero permutation importance. Repeating it against pre-dawn temperature, when urban heat island effects are strongest, gave the same answer: −0.004.

At this resolution the data encodes where heat is, extremely well, but not why. A canopy slider would have moved a number the data cannot justify. So this tool reports exposure that was measured, and leaves causal claims alone.

Limits worth knowing

Forecasts are spatially flat. Spread across sites collapses with horizon — 0.40 °C at dawn, 0.11 °C at +3 h, 0.025 °C at +6 h. Forecasts tell you how dangerous the next few hours are; they cannot tell you which site is worse. Site ranking comes only from historical layers.

The forecast window is shorter than documented, and it shrinks. Advertised at 12 hours. Probing it at 7:31 AM reached 2 PM; probing again at 10 AM also reached 2 PM. It is a fixed afternoon cutoff, not a rolling horizon — so by mid-afternoon there is no forecast left. Past the cutoff the API returns an empty tile list with a success status rather than an error, so code that does not check tile count would read that as "no heat anywhere."

High thresholds degrade. At 43 °C the exceedance layer returned negative hours — interpolation past the edge of the data. The threshold used here sits inside the reliable band.

Incidence is assumed, not measured. Cost per case is well documented; cases per hour of heat exposure is not established in the literature. Absolute costs here are illustrative. The difference between sites is the robust output, because one assumption applies to all of them.
